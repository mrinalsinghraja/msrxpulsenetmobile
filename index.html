FUTURE. INTELLIGENCE. IMPACT.

Is my internet OK? One plain answer, a live heartbeat of your connection, a speed test and a guided check that finds the slow part. No account, no ads, no tracking.

What it does

  • Home: a one-line verdict, a live heartbeat of every check, traffic, reply time and dropped checks.
  • Speed test: download, upload, ping and jitter, with a score, what your speed is good for, and a picture you can share.
  • Wi-Fi: your network name and connection type, and signal strength where iOS shares it.
  • Find problem: checks this iPhone, router, internet provider, website lookups and websites in order, then names the slow part.
  • More: history and PDF reports, data usage, a scan of devices on your network, network tools, alerts and Ask.

Questions

Why does Wi-Fi not show signal strength or security?

iOS decides what an app may read. On many iPhones it shares the network name but not signal strength or the encryption type. PulseNet then shows only what iOS reports and checks the connection by measuring it, instead of guessing.

Why does it ask for location?

Only so iOS will tell PulseNet the name of your Wi-Fi network. Your location is never stored or sent anywhere. The app works without it.

Why does it ask for Local Network access?

To check your router and, when you tap Scan, list the devices on your home network. This stays on your device.

Why are there gaps in my history?

iOS pauses apps in the background, so PulseNet records while it is open, plus a few quick checks iOS may allow in the background if Background App Refresh is on.

What is Ask?

A chat about your connection answered by Apple’s on-device model, so it needs a device with Apple Intelligence turned on. Everything else works without it.
